No vanity score. No vague advice. Just a clearer view of what is getting in the way of growth — and what to do next.

Most marketing advice is written for consumer brands. It assumes that people already understand the product, trust the company and are ready to buy.

That is rarely how wellness works.

A chiropractic and aesthetics partnership may need to work within DHA or HPCSA requirements. A biohacking or functional-medicine practice may need to prove that its promises are credible before someone will book. A clinic may have plenty of interest but still struggle to turn enquiries into appointments.

In each case, the problem is usually more specific than "we need better marketing".

Why this is different

Why the usual marketing advice often misses the mark

  • Clinical, aesthetic and wellness services can be subject to different licensing and advertising rules. A campaign that works for a beauty brand may create problems for a clinic.
  • The more ambitious the health or performance claim, the more evidence people expect to see before they trust it.
  • People rarely search for a broad category. They search for a specific problem, such as "plantar fasciitis specialist" or "IV therapy for fatigue in Dubai".
  • More traffic will not fix a business if the offer, proof or follow-up experience is the real problem.
What it covers

What you'll get from the diagnostic

The diagnostic starts with two simple questions: who needs to trust you before they buy, and what stage your business is at. It then looks at four areas:

Customer progress

Frequently asked

Questions worth answering directly

How much should a wellness clinic spend on marketing?

AmSpa (the American Med Spa Association) reports that medical spas invest an average of about 7% of revenue in marketing, with most practices somewhere between 2% and 15% depending on size, competition and growth ambitions. Developing practices still building a client base tend to sit lower, around 4–6%, while higher-performing, established practices often invest 8–12%.

That figure comes from medical spa data specifically — a chiropractic, biohacking or longevity practice may sit elsewhere on that range. There is no single percentage that works for every business. The right level depends on factors such as client lifetime value, local competition and how much demand already exists, which is exactly what the diagnostic helps clarify.

Does XL Marketing understand DHA and HPCSA requirements?

Yes. Marketing clinical and aesthetic services in the UAE and South Africa comes with specific regulatory considerations, including facility licensing, advertising rules, claims and testimonials.

Those requirements need to be considered from the start of a campaign, not checked after the work is finished.

Is the diagnostic only for clinics?

No. It also applies to biohacking, longevity and performance-health businesses.

The questions cover customer needs, trust, evidence, the client experience and commercial priorities. The opening questions then adapt the results to the business model.

What happens after I complete it?

You will see your strongest area, the main constraint affecting growth and the next step worth taking before you spend more on marketing.
