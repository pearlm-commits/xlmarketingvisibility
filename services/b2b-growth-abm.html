Frequently asked

Questions worth answering directly

What is account-based marketing?

Account-based marketing treats a named set of high-value accounts as a market of one, aligning research, messaging, content and engagement to the specific buying committee inside each account rather than a broad audience.

How do you select accounts for an ABM programme?

Accounts are selected using a combination of firmographic fit, commercial potential, evidence of an active or emerging buying trigger, and realistic access to the buying committee. Selection should be a joint marketing-and-sales decision, not a marketing-only list.

Does ABM replace demand generation?

No. ABM and broader demand generation typically serve different parts of a B2B portfolio — ABM for a defined set of strategic accounts, demand generation for the wider addressable market. Most B2B organisations need both, allocated deliberately rather than by default.

How does executive engagement fit into B2B growth?

Complex B2B sales usually involve a buying committee that includes senior decision-makers. Executive engagement — briefings, relationship mapping and credible senior-to-senior conversation — is often the difference between a stalled opportunity and a progressing one.

What does marketing and sales alignment have to do with ABM?

ABM only works when marketing and sales agree on the account list, the signals that indicate progress, and who owns each stage of engagement. Without that alignment, ABM becomes a marketing activity that sales does not use.
