Frequently asked

Questions worth answering directly

What is included in a brand positioning strategy?

A positioning strategy typically includes a positioning statement, category and audience definition, value proposition, message hierarchy, proof framework and guidance for applying the position consistently across sales, marketing and leadership communication.

How do we know whether our positioning is unclear?

Common signals include customers struggling to explain why the offer is different, sales and marketing describing the business inconsistently, messaging that shifts by channel without a stable strategic idea, and content production increasing without a corresponding rise in recognition or preference.

What is the difference between positioning, messaging and a value proposition?

Positioning is the distinctive place a business occupies relative to alternatives in a customer's mind. A value proposition states the specific value it offers a defined audience. Messaging translates both into language that different audiences and channels can use consistently.

Can XL help align several business units or sub-brands?

Yes. XL has led brand consolidation and architecture work across multiple sub-brands and regional business units, clarifying which associations should stay consistent and where individual units need distinct positioning within a shared system.

How long does a positioning engagement take?

Scope depends on the decision's complexity. A focused positioning review can be completed in a matter of weeks; a full repositioning involving multiple stakeholders, markets or sub-brands takes longer. XL agrees a specific scope and timeline before work begins.
