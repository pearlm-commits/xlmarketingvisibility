The front door

Brand & Content Sprints

One important marketing problem. Senior attention. A defined finish line.

Not every business is ready for a large engagement, and not every problem needs one.

Sprints are a way to work with XL on a single marketing priority within a controlled budget. Over two to four weeks we combine focused strategic thinking with studio-grade execution and produce something the business can use straight away — a stronger market message, a cornerstone authority asset, a launch foundation, or a repair to a buyer journey that is losing people.

Every Sprint begins by clarifying the decision behind the deliverable you have asked for. We gather only the evidence that decision requires, develop the agreed work, and hand over implementation and measurement guidance. There is no long-term commitment. Scope, deliverables, timeline and investment are agreed before anything begins.

Best suited to
  • A business that needs senior marketing expertise within a set budget
  • A launch that needs stronger positioning and campaign direction
  • Messaging that no longer reflects what the business has become
  • A cornerstone content series built for search and AI-led discovery
  • A priority buyer journey or conversion problem
  • A leadership team that needs agreement before committing further budget
  • A growth decision that cannot wait for a broader change programme
Every Sprint includes
  • Focused discovery and evidence review
  • A clearly framed strategic decision
  • Senior strategic and creative direction
  • Development of the agreed market-ready deliverables
  • Design and development required for those deliverables
  • One stakeholder review and refinement cycle
  • Implementation priorities and ownership guidance
  • A concise measurement and learning plan
  • A final handover session
Timeline: 2–4 weeks   Investment: typically $6,500–$18,000, depending on complexity, evidence requirements, stakeholder involvement and production scope.

Sprints are not reduced versions of a retainer, and they are not bundles of disconnected outputs. Each one is built to solve one important problem, remove a constraint, and leave a usable result behind.

Sprint 01 — Brand Decision Sprint

From $6,500
The client situation

A rebrand is being discussed. The identity looks dated to the people who see it every day, campaigns are underperforming, and a new look is starting to feel like the answer. Nobody has yet established whether the brand is the constraint, or whether it is carrying the visible symptoms of something operational underneath.

The decision

Does this business need a rebrand, a repositioning, a refresh or a repair — and what is the smallest sufficient change that will make it easier to understand, recognise, trust and choose?

Scope includes
  • Structured review of the observed symptoms against their likely underlying conditions
  • Recognition audit of existing distinctive assets: what buyers already know, and what rebuilding it would cost
  • Positioning review across your website, LinkedIn and third-party sources
  • Evidence review covering recent wins, losses, and where buyers misclassify the business
  • Competitor positioning and category comparison
  • A written recommendation — rebrand, reposition, refresh or repair — with the reasoning and the evidence behind it
  • Scope, cost, risk and rollout sequencing for the recommended route
  • Where the recommendation is reposition or refresh: a positioning statement, a messaging framework covering audience, proposition, proof and objections, and core page copy rewritten to it
  • Implementation priorities and ownership guidance
  • Measurement baseline and 90-day learning plan
Typical outcome

A decision leadership can defend, with the cheapest sufficient version of it identified — including, where the evidence points that way, the case for leaving the brand alone.

Visual identity design and rollout are scoped separately once the decision is made.

Sprint 02 — AI Visibility & Authority Content Sprint

From $8,500
The client situation

The company knows its subject properly. Its content does not show it. What exists is scattered, hard to find, or written generally enough that search engines and AI answer platforms have no reason to cite it over anyone else's.

The decision

Which question should this brand become the credible answer to, and what evidence, expertise and structure will earn it that position?

Scope includes
  • Review of current search and AI discoverability
  • Audience-question and search-intent analysis
  • Competitor answer and content-gap review
  • Selection of one commercially relevant cornerstone topic
  • Cornerstone article strategy and information architecture
  • One researched and fully developed cornerstone article
  • Editorial design and development of that asset
  • Three supporting article briefs or derivative assets
  • On-page SEO and answer-engine structure
  • Internal-linking recommendations
  • Author, evidence, citation and trust-signal recommendations
  • Publishing and technical implementation within the agreed platform scope
  • Measurement baseline and 90-day learning plan
Typical outcome

An authority asset good enough to be cited, and a structure the rest of your content can be built onto.

After your diagnostic

The Strategic Blueprint Workshop

Your results tell you where you stand. They don't tell you what to do on Monday.

This is ninety minutes with the person who built the diagnostic, working on your business rather than the category. Before we meet, we read your site the way an AI system reads it and compare that against how you answered. The gap between the two is usually where the conversation starts.

It's a working session, not a presentation. Bring the constraint you are actually under — the budget that won't move, the team of two, the launch in six weeks — because the plan has to survive it.

You'll leave with
  • A read on your results that accounts for what we can see on your site, and what your competitors are doing that you cannot see from inside
  • A thirty-day plan your own team can run, ordered by what will change something soonest
  • The structural fixes worth making first — the ones that decide whether AI search can describe your business accurately at all
  • A clear view of where to put the next portion of budget and attention, and what to stop doing to free it up

Choosing the term

Partnerships run for 3, 4, 6 or 12 months, chosen against the objective and the time the work realistically needs to be implemented, observed and improved.

Three months

Implementing one clearly defined priority after the Diagnostic Brief: launching an authority content system, correcting a buyer journey, establishing a new operating rhythm.

Four months

A defined initiative that needs implementation followed by a first learning and improvement cycle: a market-entry campaign, a messaging rollout, a coordinated content programme.

Six months

Sustained work across several connected levers, with enough time to establish a baseline, test the market's response and improve the system.

Twelve months

Multi-market brands and platforms needing ongoing fractional leadership, cross-functional governance, capability building, and several cycles of implementation and learning.

The recommended term follows the objective and the realistic learning period. It is not a contract preference.

Scope clarity

What is included, and what is costed separately

Our proposals give you a realistic view of what implementing the agreed work will cost.

Included within the agreed scope

  • XL's strategy, advisory and project direction
  • Copywriting, content development and editorial direction
  • Design and development required for the specified deliverables
  • Agreed website and technical amendments where access permits
  • Outreach and earned placement in publications that do not charge placement fees
  • Coordination with agreed internal teams and delivery partners
  • The review, refinement and handover cycles stated in the proposal

Costed separately where required

  • Paid media planning, management and media spend
  • Paid editorial, advertorial, sponsorship or PR placement fees
  • Specialist primary research: commissioned interviews, surveys, panels, focus groups, market studies
  • Specialist third-party data or research licences
  • Translation, complex localisation, or regulatory review beyond the agreed scope
  • External software, platform, hosting or technology fees
  • Travel, production or specialist supplier costs not included in the original proposal

In South Africa, relevant editorial opportunities can often be secured without a placement fee. In the UAE and other markets where publishers require payment, any placement, sponsorship or advertorial cost is disclosed and approved before we commit to it. No third-party cost is incurred on your behalf without prior approval.
