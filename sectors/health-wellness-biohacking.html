Our perspective

A point of view on health and wellness marketing

Health and wellness marketing should not rely on urgency, fear or claims that cannot be supported.

The best brands make complex information easier to understand while respecting the buyer's intelligence. They create confidence through clarity, evidence and relevance, not through exaggeration. This is the same thinking behind our Decision-Led Growth approach, applied to a category where trust has to be earned before it converts.

Frequently asked

Questions worth answering directly

What makes health, wellness and biohacking marketing different from other categories?

Buyers in this category are often trying to make sense of complex information, competing claims, and products that sit close to regulated health categories. That changes what good marketing looks like — the job is to make a credible brand easier to find, understand and trust, not to make the boldest possible claim.

Does XL help with regulatory-sensitive health and wellness marketing?

Yes. Health, wellness and biohacking marketing sits close to regulated claims, and XL's approach starts by identifying what can be said credibly and safely in each market, rather than the most persuasive claim that might convert.

Does this apply to biohacking and longevity brands, or only clinics and practitioners?

Both. The sector spans product brands, practitioners and platforms — the shared challenge is the same: complex information, a sceptical buyer, and a category where trust has to be earned before a purchase decision is made.

What does XL actually do for a health, wellness or biohacking brand?

Brand and marketing strategy, category positioning, audience and buyer insight, value proposition and messaging, content and demand-generation strategy, AI search visibility, campaign planning, marketing and sales alignment, measurement and attribution, and fractional CMO leadership where it's needed.
